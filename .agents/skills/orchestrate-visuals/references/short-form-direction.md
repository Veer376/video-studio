# Short-Form Visual Direction

Apply this reference only when `session.json` has `visual.aspect_ratio: "9:16"` and `brief.target_duration_seconds <= 180`. It augments the normal scene-direction rules; it does not change narration, scene boundaries, timing, or the approved global design.

## Build the Visual Arc

Before writing prompts, assign each scene an internal function: hook, advance, escalate, reveal, or land. Do not persist these labels.

- Make each scene introduce a meaningful visible action, consequence, discovery, or emotional change.
- Preserve one central visual question through the sequence. Do not reveal its answer before the narration does.
- Let consecutive scenes retain a compatible composition when continuity or suspense benefits; variation is not a goal by itself.
- Use research as supporting material, especially `Anti-Hooks & Open-Loop Mining`, `Textured Details`, `Emotional Beats, Human Conflict & Silence Moments`, `Creative Bridges`, and `What the Composer Should Emphasise`. Scene text remains authoritative.

## Direct the Opening

The first image is part of the hook, not an establishing prelude.

- Show the specific tension, contradiction, consequence, or interrupted action promised by the opening narration.
- Make the primary subject, relationship, and action understandable without backstory.
- Use clear subject separation, readable pose and gesture, and one dominant focal relationship at phone size.
- Remove nonessential figures, props, scenery, and decorative detail that compete with the hook.
- Use a wide establishing view only when the setting itself creates the opening question.
- Keep essential action away from the extreme edges of the vertical frame.

## Compress Each Image

- Give each image one dominant story beat. Include every action-critical participant and prop, but minimize background information that does not advance that beat.
- Prefer concrete physical evidence over narrated concepts: a hand releasing a weapon, an unchanged distance, an empty chair, or a broken object.
- Make emotion legible through body state, gaze, distance, and interaction instead of generalized mood language.
- Use camera distance and visual contrast according to the beat; do not make every scene a close-up or manufacture constant intensity.

## Land the Payoff

- Make the revelation or decision visually unmistakable when it arrives.
- When truthful to the story, echo an opening subject, prop, position, or relationship in a changed state.
- End on the resolved action or consequence. Do not append a generic inspirational tableau after the payoff.

## Short-Form Audit

Reject and revise the sequence if:

- scene 1 needs biography or exposition before its action is understandable
- the opening image reveals the eventual answer
- adjacent scenes repeat the same visible state without increasing tension or understanding
- mobile-scale clutter weakens the principal action or relationship
- arbitrary camera, lighting, or location changes damage continuity
- the final image fails to resolve or meaningfully echo the opening promise
