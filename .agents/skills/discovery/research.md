# Discovery Research — Katha Mind Experiment #1

**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Experiment role:** Chanakya control market / image-story control format
**Goal:** Test whether evidence-first topic selection + stronger packaging + a hard first-45-second retention gate can materially outperform Katha Mind's previous long-form behavior before changing the production medium.

## User Direction

Create the first controlled Katha Mind long-form experiment using the existing `image-story-studio` pipeline. Do not rebuild the automation yet. Keep the production format as image story so the strategic variables can be tested without simultaneously introducing generated video.

The research must start from observed viewer pain and actual YouTube behavior, not from seeded autocomplete scores or the old synthetic V-score as ground truth.

## Search Plan

1. Direct YouTube search for Hindi long-form videos around:
   - being taken lightly / losing respect
   - humiliation / insult
   - enemies without open fighting
   - silence / ignoring / emotional control
2. Compare cross-channel repeatability and recent view velocity.
3. Read locally collected competitor hooks/transcripts for high-performing Chanakya videos.
4. Inspect comments on the strongest relevant videos for the language viewers themselves use.
5. Compare the market pattern with Katha Mind's previous `chanakya-toxic-people` long-form video and its retention.
6. Build one original story premise that serves the proven pain without remaking the previous Somdatt/toxic-people story or copying a competitor script.

### What this evidence does and does not mean

- It does **not** prove exact search demand.
- It does show repeated, independent audience response around one emotional job: **"people do not respect/take me seriously; how do I regain power without losing control?"**
- The strongest opportunity is therefore broader than "toxic people" and more concrete than generic "self-respect": **being taken lightly / humiliated + regaining respect without open confrontation.**
- Chanakya is the authority/vehicle. The underlying market is the pain.

## Selected Opportunity

### Core pain
**"People have started taking me lightly because I over-explain, over-react, stay too available, or tolerate disrespect."**

### Core transformation
From **reactive and easily dismissed** → **calm, bounded, difficult to manipulate, and respected**.

### Authority vehicle
Chanakya-inspired strategic wisdom.

### Ethical framing
Do not glorify revenge, fear, manipulation, or "dark psychology." The winning emotional energy can be retained while Katha Mind differentiates through **self-respect, boundaries, emotional control, and strategic silence**.

## Experiment #1 Content Brief

### Working title — primary packaging hypothesis
**जब लोग आपको हल्के में लेने लगें — चाणक्य की 5 खामोश चालें**

Why this title:
- names the pain in the viewer's language
- promises a finite mechanism
- uses the proven "silent power" tension
- Chanakya supplies authority
- does not copy the old "Toxic People" packaging

### Packaging alternates for the Compose round
1. **लोग आपको हल्के में क्यों लेते हैं? चाणक्य की 5 खामोश चालें**
2. **जब लोग आपकी इज़्ज़त करना छोड़ दें — चाणक्य की ये 5 बातें याद रखना**
3. **हर बात का जवाब देना बंद करो — तभी लोग तुम्हें गंभीरता से लेंगे | Chanakya Niti**

### Thumbnail hypothesis
- One dominant stern Chanakya close-up, not a crowded collage.
- Secondary visual only if legible: a dismissive/mockery gesture from another figure toward a smaller protagonist.
- Text: **"इज़्ज़त वापस लो"** (3 words).
- Emotional contrast: contempt/dismissal versus controlled authority.
- Avoid generic glowing spirituality; the click promise is interpersonal power/self-respect.

### Audience
Hindi-speaking adults interested in practical spiritual, philosophical, and motivational stories.

### Format
- Long-form horizontal image story
- 16:9
- Target: **~420 seconds / 7 minutes**
- Existing image-story production stack; no generated-video clips in this control test.
- Organic distribution only; no ads and no companion Short at initial launch.

### Viewer promise
By the end, the viewer should understand five observable behaviors that make a calm person harder to exploit or dismiss — without becoming aggressive or cruel.

### Original story carrier
Use a **fictionalized Chanakya-era parable**, clearly treated as a story rather than claimed as a documented historical episode.

Recommended carrier:
A capable young royal scribe/adviser is repeatedly interrupted, mocked, and made to perform others' work because he keeps explaining himself and trying to win approval. After one public humiliation, Chanakya does not teach him revenge. He changes the young man's five behaviors. Over the next few days the same social group encounters a different person: fewer explanations, controlled responses, limited access, explicit consequences, and visible competence. The final payoff is a second public provocation in which the protagonist does not "destroy" the antagonist; he calmly refuses the frame, produces the needed evidence/result, sets a boundary, and leaves the antagonist without leverage.

This is intentionally different from the prior Somdatt + poisonous-snake + merchant story.

### Five behaviors embedded in the plot
1. **Stop over-explaining yourself.** One clean answer, then silence.
2. **Do not react on the provocateur's timing.** Delay the response until emotion is controlled.
3. **Control access to your time and help.** Kindness is not permanent availability.
4. **State one boundary with a real consequence.** No vague threats or shouting.
5. **Let competence/results carry status.** Do not constantly announce your strength.

These should emerge through events, not as five disconnected lecture segments.

## First 45 Seconds — Hard Retention Gate

The opening is not yet the final narration. It is a production contract that Compose must satisfy.

### 0–5s — Immediate power inversion
Open on the humiliation itself, not a greeting or a question.

Conceptual line:
**"जिस दिन लोग आपकी चुप्पी को डर समझने लगें, उसी दिन आपकी अच्छाई आपके खिलाफ इस्तेमाल होने लगती है।"**

Visual: protagonist is publicly interrupted/mocked while others laugh; he starts to explain himself.

### 5–12s — Interrupt the expected reaction
Before he can defend himself, Chanakya stops him with a look/gesture.

Narrative promise: the strongest answer to disrespect is not always an immediate answer.

### 12–22s — Concrete curiosity
Show the protagonist expecting Chanakya to teach him how to retaliate. Instead Chanakya gives him a counterintuitive behavioral instruction.

The viewer should wonder: **what did he change that made the same people behave differently?**

### 22–35s — Demonstrate, don't explain
Move directly into the first test the next day. The same person tries the same disrespect. This time the protagonist does not over-explain.

### 35–45s — Open the larger loop
Establish that this is the first of five changes, with the later one carrying the greatest consequence for the antagonist.

### Opening rejection rules
Reject/rewrite the opening if any of these occur:
- more than one rhetorical question in the first 45s
- "दोस्तों..." / channel greeting / subscribe CTA
- Chanakya biography or ancient-history exposition before conflict
- abstract definition of self-respect
- more than ~8 seconds without a new narrative state, shot purpose, or information reveal
- the story protagonist has not appeared by ~10s
- the first concrete behavioral shift has not begun by ~30s
- an unverified quotation is presented as a literal Chanakya quote

## Story Architecture (~7 minutes)

### 0:00–0:45 — Cold open + first behavioral shift
Public humiliation; Chanakya prevents the reactive defense; protagonist applies first change almost immediately.

### 0:45–1:35 — Why he became easy to dismiss
Compact backstory shown through two concrete incidents, not narration-heavy biography. He confuses goodness with constant compliance.

### 1:35–2:30 — Move 2: response timing
A provocateur tries to force an emotional reaction. He delays and answers after gathering control/evidence.

### 2:30–3:25 — Move 3: access
Someone who routinely dumps work on him expects automatic help. He declines calmly and redirects responsibility.

### 3:25–4:25 — Move 4: boundary + consequence
The main antagonist crosses a clear line. Protagonist states one boundary and the consequence without threat theatrics.

### 4:25–5:30 — Move 5: results over declaration
He completes a difficult task quietly. His usefulness/status becomes visible through outcome, not speeches.

### 5:30–6:35 — Payoff confrontation
The antagonist repeats the original public tactic. This time the protagonist refuses to enter the argument, presents the relevant result/evidence, states the limit, and exits the frame. The social power changes without violence.

### 6:35–7:00 — Modern mirror / resolution
Translate the story into today's office, family, friendships, and relationships in a compact way. Final takeaway: calm is only powerful when it has boundaries.

## Visual Direction For The Image-Story Control

The experiment must not accidentally test "static slideshow" versus competitors.

For the first 45 seconds:
- every surviving scene boundary must correspond to a materially different visual state
- prefer close reaction → social humiliation tableau → Chanakya intervention → protagonist's restrained reaction → next-day repeat test
- use camera-distance changes and composition to create motion energy even though the source medium is still images
- do not hold one generic talking portrait through multiple conceptual beats

Across the full video, the existing split-script rule to audit holds over ~15 seconds is especially important. The hook should generally turn faster than the body because the viewer has not yet committed.

## Success / Failure Criteria

This is an experiment, not a declaration that Chanakya is the permanent niche.

### Primary question
Does the new strategic method produce materially better **organic long-form viewer behavior** than Katha Mind has previously achieved?

### Metrics to inspect
1. Organic impressions and traffic source mix
2. CTR once sample size is meaningful
3. 30-second audience retention
4. average view percentage / average view duration
5. Browse + Suggested + Related-video acquisition
6. likes/comments/subscribers per organic view

### Internal decision thresholds (experiment targets, not industry benchmarks)
- The old long-form is around 35% retained near 28 seconds. A useful first goal is **>50% at ~30 seconds**; >60% would be a strong signal.
- Old overall APV is 15.18%. A useful first goal is **>25% APV**; >35% would be a strong signal.
- If impressions remain extremely small, classify as **insufficient distribution evidence**, not automatically a content failure.
- If impressions exist but CTR is weak, diagnose packaging before story.
- If CTR is acceptable but first-30s collapses, diagnose hook/visual pacing.
- If early retention improves but the middle collapses, diagnose story structure and repetition.

## Rejected Directions

### Generic "Toxic People" sequel
Rejected because Katha Mind already published a conceptually similar story. It would not isolate whether the new method discovered a genuinely sharper pain.

### Pure revenge / "destroy your enemy" framing
Rejected for Katha Mind differentiation. It attracts clicks but risks making the story generic dark-psychology content and weakens the channel's practical-wisdom identity.

### Generic overthinking video
Rejected for Experiment #1 because earlier demand scoring was biased by seeded autocomplete recurrence, and the Chanakya interpersonal-power cluster currently has stronger direct behavioral evidence for a control test.

### Generated-video version immediately
Rejected as the first control because it changes the production medium at the same time as topic selection, packaging, and hook methodology.
