# Sound and continuity

Sound directs attention, establishes space, and connects actions. Continuity records the state another shot must inherit.

## Sound layers

Choose only layers that serve the scene:

1. **Speech:** dialogue, voiceover, exertion, or deliberate vocal silence.
2. **Synchronized effects:** footsteps, contact, mechanisms, cloth, objects, doors, impacts, or environmental reactions tied to visible events.
3. **Ambience:** the stable acoustic identity of the location and time.
4. **Music:** emotional structure, rhythm, escalation, release, or deliberate absence.

State the priority when layers compete. Do not use generic cinematic music or constant background noise as a substitute for scene-specific sound.

## Dialogue and acoustic space

- Preserve exact words and identify the speaker and delivery.
- Account for the listener's audible or silent state.
- Let room size, distance, surfaces, weather, and surrounding activity affect how speech feels.
- Leave enough acoustic space for an important line or reaction to register.
- Use off-screen sound only when its source and narrative purpose are understandable.

## Synchronization

Tie important sounds to visible causes: preparation, contact, resonance, and decay. A synchronized sound can mark a reveal, motivate a reaction, bridge a cut, or make physical movement feel grounded.

## Continuity in

Record only inherited state that affects the shot:

- character identity, position, orientation, eyeline, posture, and emotional state;
- location geometry, time, weather, lighting direction, and active environmental motion;
- prop ownership, count, placement, condition, and movement;
- screen direction, camera relationship, or literal opening composition when required;
- dialogue, ambience, music, or an audio tail already in progress.

Inspected visual evidence outranks assumptions about an earlier clip.

## Continuity out

Define the exact state the next shot receives:

- final composition and subject positions;
- gaze, posture, expression, and unfinished movement;
- prop and environment state;
- light, weather, and time progression;
- final spoken word, effect decay, ambience, music, or silence.

Do not force visual continuity when the story intends a new time, place, perspective, or montage transition. State the discontinuity deliberately.

## Transition choices

- **Match action:** continue one movement across the boundary.
- **Eyeline or point-of-view:** show what a character sees, then return to the response.
- **Sound bridge:** begin or continue sound across the visual transition.
- **Graphic or motion match:** connect similar shapes, direction, or movement for deliberate visual flow.
- **Hard contrast:** cut between opposed scale, sound, colour, pace, or emotion for impact.
- **Settled handoff:** finish on a stable composition when the next shot requires a clean opening anchor.

Choose a transition because it advances comprehension, emotion, rhythm, or continuity—not merely to decorate the edit.
