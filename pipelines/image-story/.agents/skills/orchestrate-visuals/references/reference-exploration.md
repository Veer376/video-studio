# Visual Reference Exploration

Use reference exploration only when the project inputs and continuity ledger leave a concrete visual question unresolved, or when a scene remains generic because its material, spatial, historical, or cinematic language is unfamiliar. Do not browse routinely for every scene.

## Choose Sources by Question

- Historical clothing, architecture, tools, and cultural objects: museum collections, institutional archives, [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/), or licensed files on [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/).
- Public-domain or reusable museum assets: [Smithsonian Open Access](https://www.si.edu/openaccess).
- Framing, composition, camera distance, lighting, and blocking: [ShotDeck](https://shotdeck.com/) or another searchable cinematography library.

## Explore with a Bounded Question

1. State the missing decision precisely, such as the construction of a river ferry, the grip used with a period weapon, or how to stage an unequal confrontation vertically.
2. Inspect a small set of relevant images and their source metadata. Search results and thumbnails alone are insufficient.
3. Extract only transferable observations: physical form, material, spatial relationship, gesture, lighting motivation, or composition principle.
4. Reconcile factual observations with `research.md`, when present, and narration. Prefer sourced project facts when references conflict.
5. Apply the answer to the scene prompt, then stop exploring.

## Boundaries

- Use references to understand and direct; do not copy an artwork, film frame, proprietary character, or identifiable production design.
- Do not add a website URL to `image_prompt` or assume production can pass remote pages to the image provider.
- Do not download, register, or reuse an external image as a character or scene asset from this step. If exact image reuse is requested, verify its licence and route it through the character-asset or production workflow.
- Cite any factual reference that materially changes a prompt when reporting the work to the user. Do not add reference URLs or exploratory notes to `scenes.json`.
- Exploration cannot override scene narration, approved design, established character identity, or continuity.
